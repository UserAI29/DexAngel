01 The premise

In every horror game, you run.
In DexAngel, something is waiting for you.

You are not the final girl. You are not the survivor with a flashlight and a bad plan. You are what the building turned into after everyone left.

Humans come inside anyway. Reporters. Investigators. Ghost-hunters with cameras, civilians with curiosity, security with something to prove. They enter your floors, your stairwells, your walls — and they start documenting.

Good. Let them take pictures. You don't chase them. You decorate for them. Bloody drag-marks that end at a sealed door. Handprints smeared across the ceiling. Torn fabric, feathers in the vents, scorch marks around the light switches, messages scratched where no one could reach. Call them aesthetics. Call them bait.

When they find your work, their personal panic climbs. Keep it high enough and they go jittery — paranoid, whispering, splitting off down dark hallways alone. Keep it below critical and nobody runs. That is your hunting window. Step out of the dark. Make a noise. Feed.

Your building.
Your prey.

  • I. You are never the victim here.
  • II. Fear is a crop. Plant it.
  • III. Too much fear and they call for help.
  • IV. Help remembers everything.

02 The Evidence System

Panic is not your meter.
It's your harvest.

Humans carry their own Panic Meters. You never see a number — you watch it in how they move. Every trace you plant pushes them up. You decide how far.

Sweet spot

Jittery. Paranoid. Split from the group. YOU MAY HUNT.

Noise lures work best right here. This is the win zone — the only place you're meant to feed.

Low panic — they stay boring

They keep the lights on. They stay in groups of four and complain about the smell. You could take one, but three witnesses are watching the door. Wait. Keep decorating.

Sweet spot — they volunteer

They argue. They split up to "check on each other". One takes the stairwell alone. One stays behind with a flashlight that flickers. None of them will be missed down here.

Critical panic — they leave

Doors. Stairs. Car keys. They bolt with your evidence clutched to their chests, and every one of them is now a witness with a story. Fail pressure starts the moment they reach the street.

Push past the line and the building empties itself. Then the sirens come — and sirens bring uniforms who read your work for clues.

03 The hunt loop

Four moves. Then dinner.

  1. 01

    Plant traces

    Move unseen. Bleed the walls. Drag-marks, handprints, torn fabric, feathers, scorch marks, scratched messages. Choose where they get found — and by whom.

  2. 02

    Watch panic climb

    They discover your work. Personal panic rises. Voices get shorter, footsteps get faster, and somebody starts asking to leave.

  3. 03

    Hit the sweet spot

    High, not critical. Jittery, paranoid, split up. The group shatters and stragglers peel off down dark corridors. That is the win zone.

  4. 04

    Lure, scare, feed

    Drop a noise where nobody should be. Stalk the jittery ones. Jump the dark. Eat — then leave new evidence for the next poor soul to find.

04 Escalation

Every wave gets harder.
So do they.

  1. Level 01 — Quiet floor

    Lone stragglers, generous timers

    A half-lit floor, a night caretaker, two curious neighbors. Learn the building. Learn what your traces do to a slow mind.

  2. Mid-game — Resistance

    Security, radios, armed civilians

    Bigger waves. Tougher types: guards who walk in pairs, residents who carry. Panic decays faster, timers shrink, groups hold together longer.

  3. Late game — Police response

    They bring warrants and rifles

    Professionals arrive who treat your aesthetics as evidence. They photograph, bag, and study everything you so carefully arranged.

  4. Fail state — The Return

    Too many escape. They come back armed.

    Let humans leave at critical panic and they leave traumatized — and armed. They return with your floor plans, your habits, your name in a file.

  5. Final boss — The Reckoning

    Everyone you let go comes back at once

    Every escape you allowed becomes one rifle in one room. An organized, coordinated strike team — fighting you simultaneously, in your building, on your floor.

05 Features

What you get behind the walls.

  • First-person monster movement

    No victim stamina. No hiding in lockers. You move like something that owns the dark — fast, silent, vertical, patient.

  • The Evidence System

    Plant disturbing "aesthetics" — drag-marks, handprints, torn fabric, feathers, scorch marks, scratched messages — exactly where they'll do the most damage.

  • Dynamic panic AI

    Every human carries a personal Panic Meter: calm crowds, jittery split-ups, critical flight. Groups react to each other and to what they find.

  • Noise & jump-scare toolkit

    Lures that pull them where you want them. Stalk mechanics to close distance. Jump scares that shatter a jittery nerve — then you feed.

  • Escalating difficulty

    More humans per wave. Faster panic decay. Security and armed civilians. Tighter timers. And eventually, the police response.

  • The boss finale

    The building keeps score. Everyone you failed to keep inside returns together, armed and organized, for one simultaneous fight.
